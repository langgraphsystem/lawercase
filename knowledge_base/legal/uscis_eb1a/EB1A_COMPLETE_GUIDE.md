## 1. Overview

### What is EB-1A?

**EB-1A (Employment-Based First Preference, Category A)** is an immigrant visa classification for individuals with **extraordinary ability** in one of five areas:

- Sciences
- Arts
- Education
- Business
- Athletics

### Key Benefits

| Benefit | Description |
|---------|-------------|
| **Self-Petition** | No employer/sponsor required |
| **No Labor Certification** | Skip PERM process |
| **No Job Offer Required** | Can petition independently |
| **Priority Date Currency** | Usually current for most countries |
| **Premium Processing Available** | 15 business day adjudication |

### Legal Basis

- **Statute:** INA § 203(b)(1)(A)
- **Regulation:** 8 CFR § 204.5(h)

## 2. Eligibility Requirements

### Three Main Requirements

1. **Extraordinary Ability**
   - Sustained national or international acclaim
   - Recognized as being at the top of the field

2. **Intent to Continue Work**
   - Must intend to continue working in the area of expertise in the U.S.

3. **Substantial Benefit to the U.S.**
   - Entry must substantially benefit the United States

### How to Prove Extraordinary Ability

**Option A:** Meet at least **3 of the 10 regulatory criteria** (see Section 3)

**Option B:** Provide evidence of a **one-time achievement** (major internationally recognized award)
- Examples: Nobel Prize, Pulitzer Prize, Oscar, Olympic Medal

## 3. 10 Evidentiary Criteria

*Reference: 8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)*

### Criterion 1: Awards
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(i)**

Documentation of nationally or internationally recognized prizes or awards for excellence in the field.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Award certificates
- Letters from awarding organizations
- Evidence of selection criteria
- Information about award significance

**Key Factors:**
- National/international recognition of the award
- Criteria for receiving the award
- Number of recipients
- Significance in the field

---

### Criterion 2: Membership in Associations
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(ii)**

Membership in associations requiring outstanding achievements, as judged by recognized experts.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Membership certificates
- Organization's membership requirements
- Evidence that members are evaluated by experts

**Key Factors:**
- Outstanding achievement requirement (not just payment/tenure)
- Evaluation by recognized national/international experts
- Distinguished nature of the organization

---

### Criterion 3: Published Material About You
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(iii)**

Published material about the beneficiary in professional or major trade publications or major media.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Articles about the beneficiary
- Title, date, author of the material
- Circulation/readership data
- Translation if not in English

**Requirements:**
- Material must be **about** the beneficiary (not just mentioning)
- Must relate to work in the field
- Must be in professional publications or major media

---

### Criterion 4: Judging
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(iv)**

Participation as a judge of the work of others in the same or allied field.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Invitation letters to review
- Proof of completed reviews
- Copies of review requests
- Thank you letters from journals/organizations

**Key Point:** Must show **actual participation**, not just invitation

---

### Criterion 5: Original Contributions
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(v)**

Original scientific, scholarly, artistic, athletic, or business-related contributions of **major significance** in the field.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Patents with evidence of significance
- Expert letters explaining impact
- Citation analysis
- Evidence of adoption by others
- Media coverage of contributions

**Key Factors:**
- Originality of the contribution
- Major significance to the field (not just any contribution)
- Recognition by experts
- Impact on the field

---

### Criterion 6: Scholarly Articles
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(vi)**

Authorship of scholarly articles in professional or major trade publications or other major media.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Copies of published articles
- Evidence of peer review
- Citation counts
- Journal impact factors

**Key Factors:**
- Published in professional/trade publications
- Scholarly nature (original research, references, expert audience)
- Evidence of circulation/reach

---

### Criterion 7: Artistic Exhibitions
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(vii)**

Display of work at artistic exhibitions or showcases.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Exhibition catalogs
- Photographs of displayed work
- Invitations/announcements
- Reviews of exhibitions

**Note:** Limited to **artistic** exhibitions; not applicable to all fields

---

### Criterion 8: Leading/Critical Role
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(viii)**

Leading or critical role for organizations with a **distinguished reputation**.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Employment contracts showing title/role
- Organizational charts
- Letters from supervisors describing role
- Evidence of organization's reputation
- Awards/rankings of the organization

**Key Factors:**
- **Leading role:** Leadership position with corresponding duties
- **Critical role:** Contribution of significant importance to outcomes
- **Distinguished reputation:** Organization must have established prestige

---

### Criterion 9: High Salary
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(ix)**

High salary or significantly high remuneration relative to others in the field.

**Evidence Examples:**
- Pay statements
- Tax returns
- Employment contracts
- Comparative salary data (BLS, industry surveys)

**Key Factors:**
- Comparison must be to others **in the same field**
- Geographic and currency considerations
- Prospective offers can qualify

---

### Criterion 10: Commercial Success
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(3)(x)**

Commercial success in performing arts (box office receipts, record sales, etc.)

**Evidence Examples:**
- Box office receipts
- Record/album sales data
- Streaming statistics
- Ticket sales

**Note:** Primarily for performing artists; may not apply to all fields

---

### Comparable Evidence
**8 CFR 204.5(h)(4)**

If standard criteria do not readily apply to the beneficiary's occupation, comparable evidence may be submitted.

**Requirements:**
- Explain why criteria don't readily apply
- Show evidence is comparable in significance
- Evidence must demonstrate extraordinary ability

## 4. Two-Step Analysis

*Established by Kazarian v. USCIS, 596 F.3d 1115 (9th Cir. 2010)*

### Step 1: Initial Evidence Review

USCIS determines whether evidence meets **at least 3 criteria** (or comparable evidence).

- Uses "preponderance of evidence" standard
- Evaluates quality of evidence within criterion requirements
- Does NOT yet determine if at "top of field"

### Step 2: Final Merits Determination

If Step 1 is satisfied, USCIS evaluates **totality of evidence** to determine:

- Sustained national or international acclaim
- Whether beneficiary is among the **small percentage at the very top** of the field

**Additional Factors (especially for STEM):**
- Publications in high-impact journals
- High citation counts / h-index
- Employment at top research institutions (R1/R2 universities)
- Invited presentations at recognized conferences
- Named grants (PI on NIH, NSF, SBIR)

## 7. Premium Processing

### Eligibility

EB-1A petitions are eligible for premium processing under classification **E11**.

### Processing Guarantee

Within **15 business days**, USCIS will:
- Issue approval notice, OR
- Issue denial notice, OR
- Issue Request for Evidence (RFE), OR
- Issue Notice of Intent to Deny (NOID), OR
- Open fraud investigation

### Timer Reset

If RFE or NOID is issued, the 15-day timer **resets** when response is received.

### Fee Refund

If USCIS fails to meet the 15-day guarantee, the premium processing fee is refunded (but processing continues).

## 8. Filing Process

### Step-by-Step Process

1. **Gather Evidence**
   - Collect documentation for at least 3 criteria
   - Obtain expert recommendation letters
   - Prepare translations if needed

2. **Complete Form I-140**
   - Part 1: Petitioner information
   - Part 2: Select "1.a. Alien of Extraordinary Ability"
   - Part 3: Beneficiary information
   - Part 8: Signature

3. **Prepare Payment**
   - Complete G-1450 (credit card) or G-1650 (ACH)
   - Calculate total: I-140 fee + Asylum Program Fee (+ Premium if applicable)

4. **Assemble Package**
   - Payment form
   - G-1145 (optional)
   - G-28 (if applicable)
   - I-140 form
   - Supporting documentation
   - I-907 (if premium processing)

5. **Submit**
   - **Online:** my.uscis.gov (standalone I-140 only)
   - **Mail:** USCIS Lockbox per filing addresses

### Filing Addresses

Check current addresses at:
https://www.uscis.gov/forms/all-forms/direct-filing-addresses-for-form-i-140-immigrant-petition-for-alien-worker

## 9. Family Members

### Derivative Beneficiaries

| Relationship | Status Code | Requirements |
|--------------|-------------|--------------|
| Spouse | E-14 | Legally married |
| Children | E-15 | Under 21, unmarried |

### Process

After I-140 approval (and when visa is available per Visa Bulletin):
- File I-485 (if in U.S.) for adjustment of status
- Or consular processing at U.S. Embassy abroad

## 11. Document Checklist

### Required Documents

- [ ] Completed Form I-140
- [ ] Payment (G-1450 or G-1650)
- [ ] Passport copy (bio page)
- [ ] Evidence for at least 3 criteria

### Recommended Documents

- [ ] Cover letter summarizing the case
- [ ] Curriculum Vitae / Resume
- [ ] Tabbed exhibit list
- [ ] Expert recommendation letters (3-6)
- [ ] Evidence of intent to work in field
- [ ] Evidence of benefit to U.S.

### Optional Documents

- [ ] Form G-1145 (e-notification)
- [ ] Form G-28 (if using attorney)
- [ ] Form I-907 (premium processing)

### Evidence Organization Tips

1. **Tab all exhibits** with clear labels
2. **Create exhibit list** with description of each document
3. **Translate** all non-English documents
4. **Highlight** key portions of lengthy documents
5. **Group by criterion** for easy reference
